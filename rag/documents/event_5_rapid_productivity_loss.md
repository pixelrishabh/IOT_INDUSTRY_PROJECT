# Rapid Productivity Loss (Skin / Formation Impairment) - Event 5

## 1. Event Name & Definition
- **Event Identifier:** Event 5 (Transient: 105) - Rapid Productivity Loss
- **Category:** Near-Wellbore Reservoir Impairment / Inflow Restriction
- **Severity Level:** High Economic & Production Impact

## 2. Physical Description & Meaning
Rapid productivity loss refers to an unexpected, steep deterioration of the well's Productivity Index ($PI = \frac{q}{P_{res} - P_{wf}}$) over a short duration (hours to days). Unlike normal long-term reservoir depletion, rapid loss indicates sudden mechanical, chemical, or physical obstruction occurring in the perforations, downhole sand screen mesh, gravel pack, or immediate near-wellbore rock pore matrix.

## 3. Typical Sensor Symptoms
- **P-PDG (Downhole Flowing Pressure):** Decreases significantly during flow at fixed choke opening (increased bottomhole drawdown required to pull fluid), OR shows severe drawdown slope while surface rate drops.
- **P-TPT & P-MON-CKP:** Steady decline matching reduced total fluid delivery to surface.
- **T-TPT / T-JUS-CKP:** Continuous decline as flow velocity decreases, resulting in greater heat loss to ambient seabed.
- **Flowrate ($Q_{oil}$, $Q_{gas}$):** Progressive, sharp reduction across test separators despite unchanged choke opening ($ABER-CKP$).

## 4. Possible Root Causes
1. **Sand / Fines Migration & Screen Plugging:** Mechanical collapse of formation sand arches or mobilization of clay fines plugging the sand control screens (gravel pack / premium mesh).
2. **Asphaltene / Wax Precipitation:** Rapid pressure/temperature drop near the sandface crossing the Asphaltene Precipitation Envelope (APE), depositing heavy organic flocculates in pore throats.
3. **Severe Near-Wellbore Emulsion / Water Block:** Heavy emulsion blocking porous permeability paths.
4. **Near-Wellbore Scaling:** Barium sulfate ($BaSO_4$) or Calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$) precipitating rapidly across perforation tunnels.

## 5. Diagnostic Verification Steps
1. Plot inflow performance relationship (IPR) and compute current $PI$. If $PI$ drops drastically at constant static reservoir pressure, downhole skin factor ($S$) has increased.
2. Review bottomhole temperature against wax appearance temperature (WAT) and asphaltene onset pressure (AOP).
3. Check sand detection acoustic monitors on the topside flowline for sand production spikes preceding the plugging event.

## 6. Recommended Operator Actions
1. Avoid aggressively opening the production choke, as increased drawdown exacerbates sand mobilization and asphaltene precipitation.
2. Prepare matrix acid stimulation (e.g., mud acid / HCl) for inorganic scale/fines or aromatic solvent soaking (xylene/toluene) for asphaltene removal.
3. If sand screen collapse is diagnosed, plan workover or downhole expandable sand screen (ESS) installation.

## 7. Critical Safety Considerations
- Forcing production under high drawdown with plugged screens can lead to catastrophic screen erosion and subsequent sand blast erosion on surface chokes and bends.
- Chemical solvent/acid treatments involve high-pressure coiled tubing operations with hazardous chemical handling risks.

## 8. Relevant Sensor Relationships
- $P_{PDG} \downarrow$, $P_{TPT} \downarrow$, $T_{TPT} \downarrow$, and $Q_{total} \downarrow$ with constant $ABER-CKP$.
- Tubing pressure drop decreases due to reduced frictional loss at lower flow velocities.
